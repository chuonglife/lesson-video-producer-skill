Part one.
Six principles.
For each one I give you a warning sign, so you can check your own videos.
Here are the six.
One video teaches one idea.
Pictures add to your words, they do not repeat them.
Cut everything that does not serve the objective.
Point at exactly what you are talking about.
Talk to one person, as if they were sitting next to you.
And make the learner do something, not just listen.

Part two.
The shape of a single video.
The open has three jobs, and thirty seconds to do them.
A hook, a mistake everyone makes, so the learner wants to keep watching.
A promise, what they can do by the end of the video.
And the route, the two or three stops ahead.
No long hello. No life story.
Here is how the time splits.
The open takes about ten percent.
The body takes three quarters, two to four ideas, each with an example and a pause.
The close takes the last fifteen percent, three takeaways and one thing to do next.
Part three.
Six steps to a finished script.
Six steps.
Fix the objective in one sentence.
Cut the lesson into eight to fifteen segments.
Write what you will say, in spoken words.
Pair each segment with one slide.
Add at least two pauses where the learner does something.
Then read it out loud and time it.
